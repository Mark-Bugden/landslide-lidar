## D11 · Patch geometry and the patch rule

- **Decision.**
  - A patch is centred on pixel corner (r, c) of its sheet raster and covers pixel rows r − 75 … r + 74 and the same
    columns (P1-04's `landslide.dtm` convention). Its square is [x − 150, x + 150] × [y − 150, y + 150] m around
    the corner's world position (x, y) = `corner_xy(transform, r, c)`.
  - **Patch rule** (Appendix A §4): the square is inside its own sheet's polygon (`covered_by`, closed), and its
    distance to every stage-2 sheet polygon is > 150 m. "Touches the stage-2 sheet buffered by 150 m" is read as
    distance ≤ 150 m, the exact definition of the buffer (no polygonal buffer approximation).
  - The 150 m stage-2 buffer is half a stage-2 window, i.e. `patch.size_m / 2` (Appendix A §4: "so stage-2 windows
    (centre inside the sheet) never overlap a stage-1 patch"). It has no config key; `sampling_params` reads
    `replication.sampling.stage2_buffer_m` if the config ever gets one, otherwise it uses `patch.size_m / 2`.
- **Why.** PLAN.md D11, Appendix A §4.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D11a-new · How the patch rule is evaluated exactly

- **Decision.** No raster approximation is used anywhere; every screen is exact up to floating-point rounding
  (~1e-10 m), and the final checks re-test the selected samples with shapely on the actual squares.
  - **Inside the sheet.** The set of centres whose square lies inside the sheet polygon P is
    P ⊖ S = P \ (∂P ⊕ S), where ∂P ⊕ S is the union over P's edges of the hexagon swept by the square S along the
    edge (the convex hull of the square at both ends). This is an exact polygonal construction (`erode_square`) and
    holds for non-convex polygons too. All 860 sheets happen to be convex quadrilaterals [E, sheets.parquet], for
    which it equals the intersection of P's four translates (tested).
  - **Corners in a polygon.** The corners strictly inside a polygon are found by an even-odd scanline on the corner
    lattice (`row_intervals`): for every corner row, the crossings with the ring edges (half-open in y) are sorted
    and paired, giving column intervals. Sets of corners are kept as row intervals, intersected and expanded only
    when needed.
  - **Stage-2 sheets.** dist(c + S, Q) = dist(c, Q ⊕ S) because S is symmetric, so a corner breaks the stage-2 rule
    iff it lies within 150 m of the exact polygon Q ⊕ S = Q ∪ (∂Q ⊕ S). This is tested with GEOS `dwithin` per
    corner, only for sheets with a stage-2 sheet within reach.
- **Why.** The rules must be exact on what is selected, and the positive feasibility count needs every corner of
  every polygon (about 240 million corners inside the eligible stage-1 polygons [E, polygon areas]; 553 million in
  their bounding boxes), which rules out per-corner shapely tests.
- **Alternatives.** Rasterising with GDAL (centre-in-polygon, with its own rounding conventions) or a distance
  transform with a band re-check. Both are approximate screens that need a second exact pass. Not needed.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] `tests/test_sampling.py`: the scanline equals GEOS `contains_xy` on every corner of
  a 301 × 351 lattice for a rotated rectangle, a star, a polygon with a hole, a multipolygon and a polygon partly
  outside the grid (one chunk and many chunks); the erosion equals `covered_by` of the actual squares for a convex
  and an L-shaped polygon; the Q ⊕ S distance equals `dwithin` of the actual squares at 4,000 random points; the
  combined sheet rule equals brute force on a 500 × 600 lattice with a stage-2 neighbour.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D12 · Positive points

- **Decision.** For each eligible stage-1 polygon (P1-08's eligibility and polygon → sheet assignment), the
  candidate corners are those strictly inside the polygon (its delivered geometry, or the repaired copy for the 68
  invalid ones, as in P1-08 D05a) that satisfy the patch rule in its assigned sheet. k = clip(floor(area / 40,000
  m²), 1, 3), with `area_m2` from `polygons.parquet` (D05). The points are drawn greedily at random: each draw is
  uniform among the corners still ≥ 100 m from every point drawn so far (exact integer distances on the lattice); if
  none is left, the polygon gets fewer than k points ("reduced if there isn't room"). Polygons are processed in
  objectid order within the sheet, drawing from the sheet's child of the `positive_points` stream (D02a-new).
- **Why.** PLAN.md D12; Appendix A §5.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] brute-force feasibility of every synthetic polygon, spacing ≥ 100 m, k ≤ k_target.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D12a-new · Positive feasibility: the patch rule only

- **Decision.** A polygon is *infeasible* when no corner strictly inside it satisfies the patch rule. Validity
  (0 m border cells, NaN) is not part of feasibility, and neither is any distance rule.
- **Why.** Appendix A §12 deviation 12 defines the 870: "Their 300 m square cannot lie inside their own sheet, or
  it would touch the stage-2 buffer". Validity is a later filter (D13a-new).
- **Deviation?** No.

## D13 · Water-filling

- **Decision.**
  - **Pools.** Flysch and non-flysch stage-1 sheets (the sheet's `flysch` flag). The flysch budget is
    round(`max_pos` × flysch share of the eligible stage-1 polygons), rounding half up; the non-flysch budget is the
    rest, so they sum to 4,000. The shares count all eligible stage-1 polygons, infeasible ones included ("in
    proportion to their eligible polygons").
  - **Order.** In each sheet, the polygons with at least one point, sorted by objectid, permuted by the sheet's child
    of the `waterfill` stream. The order does not depend on the cap.
  - **Cap.** For a cap K, each sheet takes its polygons in that order, each one whole (all its points) if the sheet's
    points stay ≤ K, otherwise skips it and tries the next. The pool total is the sum over its sheets. Every K from 1
    to the largest per-sheet point total is evaluated, and the largest K whose total is ≤ the pool budget is used
    (this is literal even if the total were not monotone in K).
  - The summary reports, per pool, the budget, the cap, the total at the cap and at cap + 1, and the totals for
    every cap.
- **Why.** PLAN.md D13; Appendix A §5 and §12 deviation 11 ("raising either cap by one would overshoot the budget").
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] `test_waterfill_budget_split_and_largest_cap`, `test_fill_sheet_takes_polygons_whole
  _and_skips`; in the synthetic world, per-sheet points ≤ cap and the total at cap + 1 exceeds the budget.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D13a-new · Validity of the positives: a filter after selection, no top-up

- **Decision.** After water-filling, each selected point is checked on its sheet raster: every pixel of the patch
  plus its 28 px channel context (the 206 × 206 px box centred on the corner, `replication.channels.context_px`)
  must be valid (not NaN after the invalid rule: z ≤ 0 or z > 2,000 m). Points that fail are dropped and counted,
  before matching. Nothing replaces them.
- **Why.** Appendix A §5 "Storage" ("checked with summed-area tables before matching") and §12 deviation 11
  (3,613 selected, 29 dropped because their patch context touched 0 m border cells, no top-up round).
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] the synthetic world has a border corner and invalid holes; dropped positives never
  reach the samples and the counts add up (`test_waterfill_and_validity_order`).
- **Deviation?** No.

## D14 · Random-negative candidates

- **Decision.** Per stage-1 sheet: grid corners r, c = 0, 30, 60, … px (60 m) over the whole raster, anchored at the
  raster origin (corner (0, 0)), row-major. Each grid point is moved by U(−15, 15) m in x and in y
  (`replication.negative_jitter_m`) and snapped to the nearest corner, which is a uniform offset of −7 … 7 px per
  axis. The jitter is drawn for every grid point of the raster, from the sheet's child of the `negative_jitter`
  stream, so the draws do not depend on the filters. A candidate must then pass, in order: inside the raster, the
  patch rule, full validity of the patch plus context, centre ≥ 150 m from every register polygon, centre ≥ 150 m
  from every ZABAGED layer 129 feature (D14a-new).
- **Why.** PLAN.md D14; Appendix A §5. Validity applies at candidate level for negatives (Appendix A §5 "Storage":
  validity is checked before matching).
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] the random candidates of every synthetic sheet equal the brute-force set (actual
  squares, raster windows, exact distances to every polygon) exactly.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D14a-new · The distance rules

- **Decision.** "≥ 150 m" excludes a centre whose exact GEOS distance to a geometry is < 150 m (150 m exactly is
  allowed). The register rule uses all 21,356 polygons of `polygons.parquet` (any `skupina`, `in_mapped` or not),
  with the 68 invalid ones repaired (P1-08 D05a), so point-in-polygon cases give distance 0. Layer 129 features are
  repaired the same way (P1-07 reports none invalid). Distances are computed exactly (STRtree `dwithin` query, then
  `distance` on the pairs).
- **Why.** Appendix A §5, config `neg_min_dist_to_polygon_m` and `neg_exclude_zabaged_landslide_m`.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D15 · Look-alikes

- **Decision.**
  - **Sources.** Layers 118 (quarry), 119 (spoil heap) and 130 (rock formation) give area candidates; layers 79
    (road) and 75 (railway) give the "cut" type. P1-07 reports that all features of 118, 119, 130 and 129 are
    polygons, so **layer 130 has no point geometries** and D15's "point geometries in layer 130 are used as points"
    case does not arise. The code still handles point features (snapped to the nearest corner) and counts any other
    geometry type as unused.
  - **Feature → sheet (new).** Each area feature is assigned to the sheet holding the largest part of its area
    (P1-08's `assign_to_sheets`, all 860 sheets, ties to the lowest sheet number), the same rule Appendix A §4
    gives for register polygons. Its candidates are drawn only inside that sheet, and only if it is a stage-1 sheet.
    This makes "up to 2 points per polygon" a per-polygon rule even though features cross sheet edges whole.
  - **Area candidates.** The corners strictly inside the feature that pass every candidate rule (D14's rules; for
    negatives, validity applies at candidate level). Up to `lookalike_max_per_polygon` = 2 of them are drawn
    uniformly without replacement (the sheet's child of the `lookalike_points` stream; features in (type,
    objectid) order). No spacing is imposed between the two: Appendix A gives none for look-alikes, and the 150 m
    spacing of the selected negatives already prevents both from being used if they are close.
  - **Cut candidates.** Points at arc length 0, 100, 200 m, … along every LineString part of every road and railway
    feature (as delivered, deduplicated by objectid, so features are whole). A point belongs to the stage-1 sheet
    containing it. It is snapped to the nearest corner, must pass every candidate rule, and is kept if the 90th
    percentile (numpy's default linear interpolation) of slope over the 21 × 21 px window (`cut_window_px`, 42 m)
    centred on the pixel holding the point is ≥ 20° (`cut_slope_p90_deg`). Two points that snap to the same corner
    give one candidate.
  - **Quota.** round(`lookalike_frac` × kept positives), rounding half up: the positives that survive validity,
    since the number of negatives is not known before matching.
  - **Matching.** See D16.
- **Why.** PLAN.md D15; Appendix A §5.
- **Alternatives.** Points every 100 m on the merged road network (`line_merge`) instead of per feature. Per
  feature is simpler, and P1-07's features are whole, so it was preferred.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] every look-alike candidate passes the brute-force rules; ≤ 2 per area feature; every
  cut candidate's p90 recomputed ≥ 20°; a feature straddling two sheets gives candidates in one sheet only.
  P1-07 counts (not ours): 149 quarries, 46 spoil heaps, 10,500 rock formations, 9,323 road and 1,303 railway
  features. P1-07 notes that quarries and spoil heaps are scarce, so the quota will lean on rock formations and
  cuts; the rule is not changed for that.
- **Deviation?** No; the feature → sheet assignment is our reading.

## D16 · Matching

- **Decision.**
  - **Slope.** `slope_mean` is the mean of the slope channel over the 150 × 150 patch, from a summed-area table of
    `landslide.channels.full_channels(z, cfg, names=["slope"])` (the patches' code path). `slope_bin` =
    floor(`slope_mean` / 2°). Exact bin: equal bins; adjacent bin: bins differing by exactly 1 (either side, chosen
    uniformly among both sides' candidates).
  - **Levels.** 1 same sheet, exact bin; 2 same block, exact; 3 same sheet, adjacent; 4 same block, adjacent; 5 same
    stratum, exact; 6 same stratum, adjacent (Appendix A §5).
  - **Look-alikes first.** For level 1, 2 and 5 in turn (exact bin only), while fewer than the quota are matched:
    the still-unmatched positives are put in random order; the types take turns in config order (quarry, spoil,
    rock, cut); on its turn a type is matched to the first positive in that order that has an available candidate
    of the type in the level's scope and exact bin, chosen uniformly among them; a type with no such positive
    leaves the rotation for that level. So the types are balanced as far as their candidates allow.
  - **Random negatives.** For level 1 … 6: the unmatched positives in random order each take a uniformly chosen
    available random candidate in the level's scope and bin(s).
  - **Spacing.** "Available" means not yet selected and ≥ 150 m from every selected negative (exact integer
    distances on the national lattice; 150 m exactly is allowed).
  - **Randomness.** One `matching` stream, consumed in this order.
  - Positives without a match are dropped and counted (by stratum).
- **Why.** PLAN.md D15 and D16; Appendix A §5.
- **Alternatives.** Each positive (in random order) trying the type whose turn it is, then the others. It balances
  types less well when one type is scarce.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] hand-built cases: each level and scope reached exactly when it should; a candidate
  148 m from a selected one is unavailable, one at 150 m is available; the round-robin gives quarry, rock, cut,
  quarry with spoil leaving (no exact-bin candidate); look-alikes are never matched at adjacent levels.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D17 · Background

- **Decision.** The random candidates still available after matching (not selected, ≥ 150 m from every selected
  negative) are put in random order (`background` stream); each is taken if it is still ≥ 150 m from the background
  taken so far, until `stage2.background_n` = 4,000. Look-alike candidates are not used.
- **Why.** PLAN.md D17; Appendix A §5 ("unused random-negative candidates, drawn uniformly"); config
  `stage2.background_n`.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D02a-new · Per-sheet random streams

- **Decision.** Per-sheet steps (positive points, the water-filling order, the negative jitter, look-alike points)
  draw from `SeedSequence([sampling.seed, stream_id], spawn_key=(sheet code,))`, where the sheet code is the sheet
  number's digits (`14-34-07` → 143407). Global steps (matching, background) use `SeedSequence([sampling.seed,
  stream_id])` directly, as `landslide.config.rng_for`. The seed is `sampling.seed` = 11; the streams are the
  registered `positive_points`, `waterfill`, `negative_jitter`, `lookalike_points`, `matching`, `background`.
- **Why.** A sheet's draws then do not depend on which other sheets exist or the processing order.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] removing one stage-1 sheet leaves every other sheet's points and candidates
  unchanged (`test_per_sheet_draws_do_not_depend_on_other_sheets`).
- **Deviation?** No; a refinement of D02.

## Dnew-samples · The samples table

- **Decision.** `samples.parquet` has the PLAN.md §4.3 columns (`id, label, x, y, row, col, sheet, kraj, flysch,
  source, polygon_objectid, fold, group_b, block, stratum, slope_mean, slope_bin, match_level, pair_id`) plus two:
  `source_layer` and `source_objectid` (the ZABAGED layer and OBJECTID of a look-alike's source feature; −1
  otherwise). `polygon_objectid` is the register objectid for positives, −1 otherwise. Every sample carries its
  own sheet's attributes; `fold` is −1 outside the flysch stage-1 sheets. Pairs are numbered in (sheet, row, col)
  order of their positive; each positive is followed by its negative; the background follows with `pair_id` −1 in
  (sheet, row, col) order; `id` is the row number. `match_level` is 0 for positives and the background.
- **For P1-10.** Use the stored `slope_bin`. Recomputing it from a per-patch slope mean could flip a bin when the
  mean lies within ~1e-13° of a bin edge. The final checks measure this (`slope_bin_flips_vs_per_patch`).
- **Deviation?** No. The two extra columns are an addition to the §4.3 contract (PLAN.md §4.3 lists the columns
  of `meta.csv`); they are flagged to the orchestrator.

## Dnew-checks · Checks asserted on every build

`check_samples` re-tests the final table independently of the build's screens, and the script writes nothing if
one fails:
- 1:1 pairs; every negative in its positive's stratum; the bin offset of each level (0 for 1, 2, 5; 1 for 3, 4, 6),
  levels 1 and 3 in the same sheet and 2 and 4 in the same block; look-alikes only at levels 1, 2, 5;
  `slope_bin` = floor(`slope_mean` / 2);
- negatives and background pairwise ≥ 150 m apart;
- every negative and background centre ≥ 150 m from every register polygon and every layer 129 feature (exact
  nearest distances, reported);
- the patch rule on the actual squares (shapely `covered_by` own sheet; no stage-2 sheet within 150 m);
  `x, y` equal to the corners (row, col); every sample on a stage-1 sheet;
- positives inside their polygon, in its sheet, eligible, and ≥ 100 m apart within a polygon;
- every patch window re-read from its raster: fully valid, and the slope mean recomputed with the per-patch code
  (`terrain_channels` on the window) equal to `slope_mean` within 1e-9°.

`--check-determinism` builds a second time in the same process and requires identical samples and summary.

## Dnew-config · Values without a config key (requests)

- **ZABAGED layer ids** (118, 119, 130, 129, 79, 75) are the brief's data-source row 6. They are module defaults in
  `landslide.sampling.ZABAGED_LAYERS_DEFAULT`, overridden by `replication.zabaged_layers` if the config has it.
  **Request to P1-16:** add `"zabaged_layers": {"quarry": 118, "spoil": 119, "rock": 130, "landslide": 129,
  "road": 79, "railway": 75}` to `replication`.
- **Stage-2 buffer** (150 m): derived from `patch.size_m / 2` (D11); optional key
  `replication.sampling.stage2_buffer_m`.
- The context (28 px), jitter (15 m), streams and background size come from existing keys.

## Performance (estimate; the real build has not run)

[E, synthetic] On a full-size synthetic sheet (2,842 × 2,457 px, deflate GeoTIFF): reading the DTM 0.19 s, the
slope raster and both summed-area tables 0.65 s, the random candidates 0.04 s; traced peak 280 MB. Pass 1 handles
an 8 km² polygon (2.0 M corners) in 0.23 s and 200 small 160-vertex polygons in 0.23 s. [I] So the real build takes
roughly 15 min (751 sheets in pass 2, plus look-alike features), the final checks a few minutes (they re-read every
sheet with samples), and `--check-determinism` doubles the build. Only one sheet raster is in memory at a time.

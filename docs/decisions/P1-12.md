## D22 · Stage-2 lattice

- **Decision.** Window centres are the pixel corners (r, c) of each reserved sheet's raster with r ≡ c ≡ 0 (mod
  `stage2.stride_px` = 25) from the raster origin (corner (0, 0)), over corners 0 … H and 0 … W. A centre is kept if
  its world position lies in the sheet polygon, closed (`shapely.intersects_xy`: the boundary counts). The window is
  the 150 px square centred on the corner (rows r − 75 … r + 74, P1-04's convention). Lattice rows are in row-major
  (r, c) order. `x, y` are `corner_xy` of the grid in `sheets.parquet`, after checking that the raster (and the
  susceptibility raster) is on the same national lattice with the same size (P1-09's check, not float equality).
- **Why.** PLAN.md D22; Appendix A §9 "150 px windows on a 25 px (50 m) lattice of each reserved sheet's DTM grid,
  centre inside the sheet".
- **Alternatives.** Strictly inside (`contains_xy`): the same 146,622 centres on the 20 sheets [E], so the choice
  makes no difference here.
- **Evidence.** [E] 146,622 windows: 73,815 on the 10 flysch sheets and 72,807 on the 10 non-flysch sheets, 7,191 to
  7,435 per sheet. PLAN.md §11 guessed about 11,000 per sheet; the true count is about the sheet area / 2,500 m²
  (about 18.4 km² per sheet). Per-sheet counts are in `data/stage2/summary.json`.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D22a-new · Windows that are not fully valid

- **Decision.** A centre whose window plus the 28 px channel context (the 206 px box, `patch.px / 2 +
  replication.channels.context_px` = 103 px on each side) leaves the raster or holds an invalid DTM cell is dropped
  from the lattice and counted (`n_invalid_dropped`). One lattice serves every scorer, so the CNN's requirement
  defines it.
- **Why.** The CNN needs all seven channels finite over the window, exactly as the training patches do (Appendix A §5
  "Validity"). Baselines must score the same windows, or the scorers would not be compared on the same detections.
- **Alternatives.**
  - Give such windows the lowest score. They would still be lattice cells and could become detections in the PR
    sweep's tail.
  - Raise an error. That would stop a smoke run on a raster with a border.
  - Score baselines on partly valid windows. The lattices would then differ between scorers.
- **Evidence.** [E] 0 windows dropped on the 20 reserved sheets. Their rasters are fully valid (P1-08), and every
  centre inside a sheet is at least 125 px from the raster edge by construction (the raster is the sheet bounds plus
  250 m). The smallest clearance measured is 129 px ≥ 103 px. `test_build_lattice_counts_and_coordinates` and
  `test_windows_valid_drops_invalid_and_outside` test the rule with holes and edges.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D22b-new · Window statistics

- **Decision.** Over the 150 × 150 window (not the context), from summed-area tables:
  - `slope_mean` and `slope_std` (ddof 0, from sums of (slope − sheet mean) and its square) of the slope channel,
    computed with `channels.full_channels(z, names=["slope"])`, the code path of the patches;
  - `susc_mean` (the mean class code 0/1/2) and `susc_high_frac` (the share of code 2) of the susceptibility raster,
    as exact integer sums.

  The slope baseline scores `slope_mean`, the susceptibility baseline `susc_mean`. All four are the LR features.
- **Why.** Appendix A §9 (window-mean slope, window-mean susceptibility class); PLAN.md D19 for the definitions; the
  card asks for SAT means.
- **Evidence.** [E] Against brute-force numpy: means to 1e-10, std to 1e-9, susceptibility exact. Against the
  per-patch channels (`patch_channels`) on synthetic rasters: slope mean and std to 1e-9.
- **For P1-10.** `meta.csv`'s `slope_std`, `susc_mean` and `susc_high_frac` must use the same definitions (ddof 0,
  the 150 × 150 patch footprint, code 2 = high), so that the LR comparator sees the same features at training and at
  stage 2.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D23 · NMS

- **Decision.**
  - Greedy, per sheet, over the lattice in descending jittered score, where jittered = score + `nms_jitter_scale`
    (1e-9) × U[0, 1).
  - A selected cell suppresses every cell at distance ≤ `stage2.nms_radius_m` (150 m). The distance is tested
    exactly on integer pixel offsets: dr² + dc² ≤ (150 / 2)² = 5,625 px², so 150 m exactly is suppressed. On the
    50 m lattice this is the 29 offsets with (Δi² + Δj²) ≤ 9.
  - Exact ties left after the jitter fall back to (row, col), and in the pooled matching to (sheet, row, col).
  - Every lattice cell ends selected or suppressed (asserted); there is no score threshold.
- **Why.** Appendix A §9 and PLAN.md D23. The jitter is additive, as "a seeded 10⁻⁹ jitter" reads. Integer offsets
  avoid floating-point noise at the 150 m edge (world coordinates such as −764,543.6 + 2c are not exact).
- **Alternatives.** A lexicographic tie-break (score, then a random key) never reorders distinct scores, but it is not
  the method's jitter. The additive jitter could reorder two scores closer than 1e-9; on our data that cannot change
  anything measurable [I].
- **Evidence.** [E]
  - Ties after the jitter: none in any lattice. The susceptibility lattice has 67,228 exact raw ties (mostly
    all-low windows at 0) and 0 after the jitter. The slope lattice has none before or after.
  - So `pr_sweep`, given the jittered scores, orders the detections exactly as the matching did.
  - Tests: the 150 m edge (75 px suppressed, (25, 75) px = 158.1 m kept), the 29-cell neighbourhood, ties broken by
    the jitter, and greedy NMS equal to an O(n²) brute force on random lattices with heavy ties.
- **Deviation?** No.

## D23a-new · Random streams per sheet

- **Decision.** The jitter and the random baseline come from `SeedSequence([replication.seed, stream_id],
  spawn_key=(sheet code,))`:
  - `replication.seed` is 2611;
  - the registered streams are `nms_jitter` (9) and `random_baseline` (10);
  - the sheet code is the sheet number's digits (`landslide.sampling.sheet_rng`, as P1-09 D02a-new).

  Each sheet draws one U[0, 1) per lattice cell in row-major order. Every scorer uses the same jitter on a sheet.
- **Why.** A sheet's jitter and random scores then don't depend on which other sheets are evaluated: the smoke test
  scores one sheet, and its draws equal those of the full run. The orchestrator named `config.rng_for(cfg,
  replication.seed, "nms_jitter")`; this is that seed and stream id plus the per-sheet spawn key.
- **Alternatives.** One `rng_for` stream drawn across the 20 sheets in a fixed order. Then a sheet's draws would
  depend on the sheets before it.
- **Deviation?** No; a refinement of D02.

## D23b-new · Object matching

- **Decision.**
  - **Targets.** The eligible polygons assigned to the evaluated sheets (`polygons.parquet` `eligible` and `sheet`):
    465 for the 20 sheets (278 flysch, 187 non-flysch) [E, equal to the spike].
  - **Order.** The detections of all evaluated sheets are processed together in descending jittered score (ties:
    sheet, row, col).
  - **TP.** The detection centre is at exact GEOS distance ≤ `match_buffer_m` (50 m) from a target that is still
    unmatched. If several qualify, the lowest objectid is matched.
  - **Duplicate.** The centre is within 50 m of one or more targets, all of them already matched.
    `target_objectid` is then the lowest of them.
  - **Ignored.** Otherwise, the centre is within `ignore_buffer_m` (150 m) of any register polygon: all 21,356,
    targets and ineligible polygons included. The 68 invalid ones are repaired with `landslide.geo.repair` (P1-08
    D05a, P1-09 D14a).
  - **FP.** Everything else.
  - The buffers are tested as exact distances (`dwithin` candidates, then `distance <= d`), not as polygonal
    buffers.
- **Why.** Appendix A §9, PLAN.md D23 ("TP: the centre lies within target.buffer(50)"; "Ignored: distance ≤ 150 m").
  Exact distances avoid the polygonal approximation of `buffer` (quad segments) at the edges.
- **Duplicate reading.** Appendix A says a duplicate is a detection "inside an already-matched target". We read
  "target" as the buffered target that defines a TP, so a duplicate is exactly a detection that would have been a TP
  had its target been free. Under the polygon-only reading, a detection in a matched target's 50 m buffer but outside
  its polygon would be "ignored" instead (it is within 150 m).
  - Both readings give the same P@R50, interpolated precision and recall, because ignored and duplicate detections
    count in neither TP nor FP.
  - They differ only in the post hoc ring-as-FP variant. [E] 373–471 of the 943–1,208 duplicates per scorer lie
    outside the polygon. Under the polygon reading the ring-as-FP P@R50 would be at least 0.079 / 0.086 / 0.074
    (random / slope / susceptibility) instead of 0.080 / 0.091 / 0.078. Those are lower bounds: only the lowest
    matched target was checked.
- **Cross-sheet matches.** Pooled targets would let a detection match a target of another sheet. On the 20 reserved
  sheets this cannot happen: the sheets are at least 4.83 km apart [E].
- **Evidence.** Hand-built tests for every outcome:
  - the lowest-objectid tie;
  - a duplicate inside a matched target and one exactly 50 m from it;
  - TP at exactly 50 m;
  - ignored at 51 m from a matched target and at exactly 150 m from a non-target;
  - FP at 150.5 m;
  - the order dependence (`test_matching_*`).

  Plus the real-data brute-force re-simulation above.
- **Deviation?** No.

## Dxx-new · Metrics, strata and the ring variant

- **Decision.**
  - `metrics.pr_sweep` gets the detections' jittered scores and outcomes, `n_targets` and
    `eval.thresholds.stage2_recall` (0.5). It returns P@R50 (primary), interpolated precision and max recall.
  - **Per stratum** (flysch / non-flysch sheets): the matching is re-run on the stratum's detections and targets
    only, and swept with the stratum's target count.
  - **Ring as FP (post hoc):** the same sweep with ignored → fp. Duplicates stay ignored, and TP still takes
    precedence over the ring. It is reported pooled and per stratum.
  - **Not reached.** If 50 % recall is never reached, the status is `not_reached`, P@R50 and interpolated precision
    are empty (NaN in the CSV), and `meets_threshold` is false. This is a FAIL (Appendix A §9).
- **Why.** Appendix A §9; P1-05's notes for P1-12.
- **Evidence.** [E] The per-stratum outcomes equal the pooled outcomes restricted to the stratum for all three
  scorers (`strata_match_pooled_outcomes` in `summary.json`), as they must with no cross-sheet matches.
  `test_evaluate_pooled_strata_and_ring` hand-computes every number of a two-sheet case;
  `test_fifty_percent_recall_never_reached_is_fail` covers the FAIL path.
- **Deviation?** No.

## Dxx-new · Logistic-regression comparator (`slope_susc_lr`)

- **Decision.**
  - **Model.** `StandardScaler` + `LogisticRegression` with sklearn's defaults (L2, C = 1, lbfgs, max_iter 100) and
    `random_state = replication.seed`.
  - **Features.** `slope_mean`, `slope_std`, `susc_mean`, `susc_high_frac`.
  - **Training rows.** Every row of `meta.csv` with `source == "positive"` (label 1) and every row with
    `source == "background"` (label 0). That is all stage-1 positives of every fold and stratum, inner-val blocks
    included, since the LR has no early stopping. Matched and look-alike negatives are not used.
  - **Score.** `predict_proba[:, 1]` of the stage-2 window features (D22b-new).
- **Why.** Appendix A §9 "logistic regression on (slope mean, slope std, susceptibility mean, high-class fraction),
  fitted on stage-1 positives vs the unmatched background sample". P1-11's card fixes StandardScaler +
  LogisticRegression with a fixed `random_state` for the stage-1 LRs. When both tasks are merged, the factory
  (`stage2.make_lr`) should be shared with P1-11's `baselines.py`.
- **Evidence.** [E, synthetic] It equals an independently built sklearn pipeline to 1e-12, and changing the matched
  negatives leaves it unchanged. Label checks raise.
- **Status.** Not run on real data: `meta.csv` does not exist yet.
- **Deviation?** No.

## Dxx-new · CNN scorer

- **Decision.**
  - **Per sheet.**
    - `channels.full_channels(z)` gives channels 0–5 in float64.
    - Windows are cut at the lattice corners, and detr is `channels.detrend` of each window's z, in float64 on the
      CPU.
    - The stack is cast to float16, exactly as `patches.npy` stores the training patches. Non-finite values or a
      float16 overflow raise.
  - **Per seed** of the `s2` run (`train.seeds`, 0–2):
    - `model.load_checkpoint(best.pt)` and that seed's `norm.json` (`predict.Normaliser.from_json`);
    - `predict.tta_predict` with chunks of `train.batch_size` (64) windows, i.e. 512 images per forward pass, under
      the training's bf16 autocast, inside `train.deterministic_mode()`.
  - **Window score.** The arithmetic mean of the seed probabilities.
  - **Refusals.** `load_s2_models` refuses a seed without `done.json`, `best.pt` or `norm.json`, or trained under
    another config hash. A missing seed makes stage 2 INCOMPLETE, never a smaller ensemble.
  - **Saved per sheet.** The per-seed probabilities go to `s2_infer/<sheet>.npz` (resume per sheet; per-seed
    spread), the ensemble to `cnn/<sheet>.npz`.
- **Why.** Appendix A §7 and §9; the orchestrator's reuse list. The network then sees, at a stage-2 window, the
  values a training patch centred on that corner would have: the same channel code (≤ 5e-9 apart in float64), the
  same detr function, the same float16 rounding and the same normalisation.
- **Alternatives.**
  - Removing the plane on the GPU, as Appendix A §9 says the spike did. This is an implementation detail. The CPU
    float64 `detrend` is the function that made the training patches' detr, so it is the more consistent choice.
  - Feeding float32 windows without the float16 rounding: a small mismatch with the training inputs.
- **Evidence.** [E, toy rasters, random-init models, CPU]
  - The windows equal `patch_channels` to 5e-9.
  - The probabilities equal a per-patch reference (patch channels → float16 → `tta_predict`) to 1e-6 at 64 px and
    150 px. Chunking doesn't change them.
  - The ensemble is the mean.
  - `run_cnn` writes both files and skips a finished sheet on rerun. Missing files and a config-hash mismatch raise.
- **Deviation?** No.

## CNN cost estimate

- **From the card's formula.** [I] 146,622 windows × 8 views × 3 seeds = 3,518,928 forward images. At P1-06's
  measured 4,600 images/s that is 12.7 min of GPU time.
- **Measured.** [E] A synthetic 2,400 × 2,850 raster, 1,536 windows, 3 randomly initialised models on the RTX 3060
  (no real data):
  - 122.5 windows/s for 3 seeds including window assembly, i.e. 2,941 images/s;
  - assembly alone 509 windows/s;
  - `full_channels` 5.6–6.6 s per sheet;
  - peak GPU memory 1.28 GiB.

  That gives about 20 min for the windows plus about 2 min for the channels. Stage-2 inference is therefore about
  22 min, well inside the 6 h cap. PLAN.md §11 assumed about 5 M forward passes; the real lattice needs 3.5 M. The
  CPU window assembly and the GPU run one after the other. Overlapping them could save about a quarter, but it isn't
  needed.

## Dxx-new · Outputs (contract additions flagged for PLAN.md §4.3)

- `data/stage2/<scorer>/<sheet>.npz`: `row`, `col`, `x`, `y`, `score` (raw, float64), exactly as §4.3. Scorers:
  `slope_only`, `susceptibility`, `slope_susc_lr`, `random`, `cnn` (the stage-1 names of Appendix A §10).
- `data/stage2/detections.parquet`: the §4.3 columns `scorer, sheet, x, y, score, outcome, target_objectid`
  (−1 when none), **plus** four more:
  - `score_jittered`, the ordering key that NMS, the matching and `pr_sweep` used, so P1-13 can recompute the sweep
    exactly;
  - `row`, `col`;
  - `rank`, the matching order.

  Rows are upserted by scorer, so the CNN's rows can be added without touching the baselines'.
- **New:**
  - `data/stage2/lattice/<sheet>.npz`: the lattice, its window statistics, `n_inside`, `n_invalid` and the config
    hash. Every scorer's lattice is checked against it.
  - `data/stage2/s2_infer/<sheet>.npz`: the per-seed CNN probabilities.
  - `data/stage2/summary.json`: per-scorer metrics (pooled, strata, ring variants), per-sheet lattice counts, the
    cost estimate, config hash and git commit.
- **Tracked `results/stage2_baselines.csv`.** One row per baseline scorer, in the order slope_only, susceptibility,
  slope_susc_lr, random. `kind` is non_ml_baseline, classical_ml or chance (Appendix A §10 terminology). The columns
  hold:
  - counts and outcomes;
  - status, P@R50, interpolated precision, max recall, the threshold at R50 and `meets_threshold`, pooled;
  - the same per stratum, with each stratum's target count;
  - ring as FP, pooled and per stratum;
  - the config hash.

  Rows of scorers that are not re-run are kept. In smoke mode the CSV goes to `data/smoke/results/`.
- **Licence.** No maps, figures or geometry are written. The tracked CSV holds only aggregate numbers (no per-sheet
  values, no coordinates). Per-sheet lattice counts and the detections' coordinates stay in `data/`.

## Observation for P1-09 / P1-10 (no change made here)

Appendix A §4 says stage-2 windows "never overlap a stage-1 patch" because patches stay more than 150 m (Euclidean)
from every stage-2 sheet. For axis-aligned squares this is not guaranteed near a stage-2 sheet's corners [I].
- A window centred at a sheet corner reaches 150 m along each axis, so its square corner lies 212 m diagonally from
  the centre.
- A patch square 150–212 m away diagonally can therefore share up to about 44 m × 44 m with it.
- Only windows centred within about 62 m (212 − 150) of the sheet boundary can be affected: the outer one or two
  lattice rings.

P1-09/P1-10 could quantify this from `samples.parquet` and the stage-2 lattice: for each patch, the smallest Chebyshev
distance to a lattice centre, overlap iff < 300 m.
